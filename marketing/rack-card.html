Everything a municipal campaign actually does

Not a rebadged federal tool. Wards you can switch off, at-large councils, and the forms your clerk is expecting.

At the door

  • Walk lists by street, with door counts
  • Log a knock in seconds — one tap for nobody home
  • Collect a mobile number with consent recorded, wording and all
  • Ask if they’ll endorse publicly — kept apart from support level
  • Take a photo together, with its own permission to publish
  • Flag a follow-up and why; it lands on a worklist
  • Add someone who isn’t on the list yet, right at the door
  • Half-finished doors are kept on the phone, not lost

On the map

  • Every address, with the doors you’ve knocked
  • Turf handed to a canvasser, and routes still to walk
  • Lawn sign locations and a run sheet for install day

Money and compliance

  • Contributions against the $1,200 per-contributor limit
  • Spending and self-funding limits from your elector count
  • Cash and anonymous rules, appreciation-party cap
  • Form 4 with Boxes A–D and Schedules 1 and 2

People and outreach

  • Volunteers, shifts and who showed up
  • Bulk text messages — only to voters who said yes
  • Events, sign requests and inventory
Running more than one candidate? A consultant can hold several campaigns across different municipalities at once, each sealed from the others — and open canvass sharing later if they run as a slate.
